## 1. Who 1.0 is for

### What MK actually used, and what it ignored

MKThink (a strategy and design consultancy, installed on its own Mac mini on 2026-07-28, still on commit `a02aaaa4`) is the only customer evidence we have.

| Used, with evidence | Not used, or never exercised |
|---|---|
| A Chief of Staff (Casper) plus a small team: research, content, ops and client-success agents (AGE-39) | **Self-serve sign-up and the v2 CoS interview.** MK was installed on site by the founder from a runbook and a claim link (`doc/customers/mkthink/00-onsite-operating-procedure.md`). The founder-facing onboarding in section 2 has never been run by a customer |
| Directing work from a phone: the principal texts a CEO agent by iMessage (AGE-39) | **Billing.** MK is on a six-month free trial, so Stripe, caps and quotas never met a customer |
| One accountable human per agent, the steward (Titus stewards Casper), which is the `agentdash_mk` product profile | Multiple companies, lead capture (#460 to #464), connectors, the Company Evaluator (built for our own dogfooding) |
| Acting away from the board: connecting Claude Code and Codex over MCP, then asking for approvals in the harness (#544), in Teams (#542, #546) and by push (#540) | The web board as the place to work. Every late MK request was a way to *not* open it |
| Hermes agents: the steward-created agent HAL on `hermes_local` (AGE-39) | On-prem as a feature to sell. MK valued "the data never leaves the office" (AGE-39), but it also produced most of the install and upgrade pain (section 7) |

### The 1.0 customer

**A founder or operator of a 2 to 15 person team that builds software or digital product, who already delegates to a personal assistant (Muse first, Grok second), has a GitHub repo, and will bring their own model API key.** They want work done while they are away from a desk, and they want to be asked only when a decision is theirs.

This differs from MK in two honest ways. MK was a consultancy doing knowledge work, not engineering, and MK wanted on-prem. What carries over from MK is the behaviour that matters: the principal directs from a phone, stewards will not live on a board, and they bring their own model. On-prem stays available as the self-hosted path, but it is not the 1.0 launch SKU.

**Not a fit for 1.0:** anyone who needs SOC 2 or a vendor questionnaire, shared tenancy, SSO, formal per-agent stewardship, or a Muse directory listing rather than a custom connector.

### The promise

> **Tell your assistant what you want built. AgentDash's agents build it, ask you only when they must, and tell you what shipped.**

### The 1.0 shape in four lines (founder, 2026-09-24)

- ~~**Invite-only.** An operator provisions each box by hand from a runbook.~~ **Self-serve (2026-09-25, supersedes D1):** a person signs up at agentdash.cloud and the control plane provisions their own box automatically, behind a waitlist with operator approval and a cap of 10 a day at launch; signup opens fully about 11-04.
- **Default product profile only.** Hosted boxes run no `agentdash_mk` profile: no stewards, no steward inbox, no bridge, no directives.
- **Hermes only.** Every hosted agent runs `hermes_local`, with a managed per-agent Hermes profile and a provider key the customer brings.
- **The built plans.** Free and Pro as built, no box fee, and idle Free boxes are suspended.

### CUJ-2: CoS interview and first hire

| CoS interview | **Built** | `/interview/turn`, `/confirm-plan`, `/revise-plan` (`onboarding-v2.ts:357,537,706`). CoS chat can dispatch through `hermes_local` (`hermes chat -q … -Q`, `services/dispatch-llm.ts:52,404`) |
| First agent hired | **Built**, with a paywall | `/agent/confirm` (`onboarding-v2.ts:438`). The Free cap is **1 agent including the CoS** (`services/tier-policy.ts:35,67`), so the first hire on Free hits the upgrade wall. **Fix, env only:** hosted boxes set `AGENTDASH_FREE_AGENT_CAP=2`, so the CoS does not use up the only Free agent. | Default profile | **Built** | New companies are `default` (`packages/db/src/schema/companies.ts:14`). A company becomes `agentdash_mk` only with an MK invite code (`routes/companies.ts:362-395`), which hosted boxes will not issue |

**The key and Hermes' state are one persistence problem.** Hermes keeps its provider credentials, profiles, session history and its token ledger (`state.db`) under `~/.hermes`, and the per-agent wrappers under `~/.local/bin`. On Railway all of that disappears on redeploy unless it lives on the Volume. One fix covers both: mount the Volume at `/paperclip`, point `HERMES_PROFILES_DIR`, `AGENTDASH_HERMES_BIN_DIR` and the Hermes home at it, and have onboarding write the customer's key into the managed template profile there. The master key and the Hermes key directory are then covered by the same backup (S1).

**Providers.** Hermes has built-in providers for OpenRouter, Anthropic, OpenAI, xAI, Gemini, Z.AI, MiniMax, DeepSeek, Kimi and Qwen, plus any OpenAI-compatible endpoint (Hermes v0.21.2). 1.0 **supports four API-key providers**: Z.AI, which is proven on the runner (glm-5.3-flash), and OpenRouter, Anthropic and OpenAI, which get proven on the box in M5. OAuth logins (Codex, xAI, Qwen) are out: a hosted box cannot hold a consumer login. MiniMax is out because Hermes reports no reliable price or usage for it (#493).

**Acceptance:** on a fresh hosted box, the claimed founder picks a provider, pastes a key, completes the interview and confirms the plan. A CoS plus one hired agent exist, each with its own Hermes profile, and complete one test task on Free. After a redeploy, the key and both profiles are still there. No operator steps in. This runs as a Playwright spec against the box.

### CUJ-6: Invite a teammate, and they see what is waiting on them

| Step | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Invite by email | **Built** | `POST /api/onboarding/invites` (`onboarding-v2.ts:861`) through Resend. Each box needs its own Resend key |
| Teammate joins | **Built** | `InviteLanding`, `MemberOnboarding`, resumable member onboarding (v2026.827.0) |
| "What's waiting on me?" | **Built**, without the steward inbox | The steward inbox, webhook push and bridge are `agentdash_mk` only (`steward-inbox.ts:130`, `routes/bridge.ts:61`), so they are **off** on hosted boxes. A default-profile teammate has two surfaces instead. **On the web:** `/approvals/pending` and `/approvals/:id` (`ui/src/App.tsx:172-175`, `pages/Approvals.tsx`) list every pending company approval and let them decide it. **In their assistant:** their own grant (CUJ-3), where `list_pending_decisions` returns approvals with a correct `canDecide` for default companies (fixed in the #685 review) plus tasks assigned to them |

**No new work for 1.0.** The assistant plus `/approvals` covers "what's waiting on me". What is lost is *push*: nobody is notified. The assistant is pull-based, so a person hears about a decision when they ask. That is acceptable for a 2 to 15 person team whose members already ask their assistant for a morning summary, and `whats_new` reports "N decisions wait for you". Revisit if the first customers miss decisions.

**Who may decide, on the default profile.** `requireDecisionActor` returns `null` for default companies, and the decision path reads that as `admin` (`services/approval-authority.ts:232`). So **any member of the company can decide any approval, except a hire**, which needs `agents:create` (`routes/approvals.ts:136-154`). **Accepted for 1.0**, on strangers' boxes, for four reasons:
1. A box holds one company, and only people the founder invites can sign in.
2. Those members *are* the board in the default model. That is how Paperclip's approvals were designed.
3. Hires, the decisions that create spend and new principals, already need `agents:create`.
4. An assistant decides only with the opt-in `agentdash:decide` scope, and authority is re-checked at `confirm_action` (spec §7.2).

The connect-your-assistant guide must say plainly: "every member can approve agent requests, except hires." The risk left over is a junior teammate's assistant approving an agent's external send. The mitigation is a per-grant "decisions need a tap" setting (spec §7.2, in M4), not new authority code.

**Acceptance:** the founder invites one teammate, who joins, connects their own assistant, asks "what's waiting on me?", and hears the pending approvals and their assigned tasks. The teammate can approve a non-hire request and cannot approve a hire.

## 4. Launch-readiness bar by the five pillars

Every gate is pass or fail, with evidence attached to #681.

### Reliable
- R1. The spec §8 twelve-step script passes **three times in a row** on the hosted launch box with Muse, within 72 hours, and each run is recorded.
- R2. `tests/e2e/assistant-mcp.spec.ts` (headless SDK client) and the hosted-box journey spec (CUJ-2, CUJ-6, CUJ-7) are green on `main` and against the box.
- R3. **No open P1** labelled `mvl-1.0` on launch day.
- R4. The token ceiling (OBS-2) is on at 5M tokens per agent per day on every box, and one pause has been observed and cleared on the launch box, and reported by `explain_blocker`.
- R5. Every Hermes run on the box in the last 7 days has a `meteringStatus` of `metered`, resolved from that agent's own profile ledger. None records a silent 0 or falls back to the root ledger (OBS-1 acceptance).
- R6. Every hosted agent runs through its own `agentdash-<id>` profile wrapper. A failed profile provision fails the run instead of falling back to the shared default.

### Understandable
- U1. Every assistant answer in the three R1 runs is 600 characters or fewer and carries a deep link that resolves on `publicBaseUrl` (automated in #681).
- U2. The redaction scanner over all recorded MCP traffic finds no `pcp_`, `pcpa_`, `pcin_`, env-shaped keys, `contextSnapshot` or adapter config.
- U3. The operator can read the running release tag of each box. Priya noted on AGE-38 (2026-09-12) that health carries no build identity; check whether v2026.924.0's Instance Updates page closes that.
- U4. `agentdash.cloud` makes no claim that the build contradicts: no dollar spend figures (the demo shows "$61.40", `ui/src/marketing/demo/scenarios.ts:121`, against the tokens-not-dollars decision on #493), and no "Start free" link into the retired instance.

### Installable
- I1. A box is provisioned automatically in **3 minutes or less** from email verification (#763), and ten concurrent signups end healthy (#772). The runbook stays the manual fallback.
- I2. The Docker image, with Hermes in it, builds on every PR (#562, merged) and the launch box runs a pinned stable image tag.
- I3. CUJ-2 passes on a fresh box with no operator help after the claim email, and the key and profiles survive a redeploy.

### Supportable
- S1. **A backup is restored on a hosted box,** covering the Postgres dump, the Volume (including the Hermes directory with its keys, profiles and ledgers) and the secrets master key. Restore time is recorded, and the restored box passes R1 steps 1 to 3.
- S2. Rollback is proven once: redeploy the previous image tag, then restore the database.
- S3. The support path is written: one support address shown as text in the app and on the site, a response target of one business day, and a runbook for the top five incidents (box down, invalid model key, ceiling or quota pause, assistant reconnect, restore). The support-access policy (D-S7: consent, time-box, audit) is decided.
- S4. The operator checks every box weekly: health, backup age, plan state and idle Free boxes. This is a runbook step, not code.

### First-customer-ready
- F1. **Security bar for hosted boxes. All of #692 and #713 to #718 are merged before the first customer box is provisioned.** In addition: #505 is fixed; #450 is closed by the hosted-box boot guard (#726) that refuses `local_trusted`; #708, #709, #710 and #719 are fixed (triage below); the #688 security review has no open HIGH or MEDIUM findings; and the `pcpa_` token reaches no raw REST route (already in #688).
- F2. `docs/guides/steward/connect-your-assistant.md` exists, states who may approve what (CUJ-6), and a first-time user follows it without help (#680).
- F3. The pricing page and Stripe prices match section 6.
- F4. **Three** named design-partner candidates who match section 1 have booked a box. MK's reference or case-study request is out (AGE-39 §4).

### Security backlog triage for 1.0

The threat model for a hosted box is set by the 1.0 shape: one customer per box, with members the founder invited, agents that read untrusted content and could be prompt-injected, a container we operate that holds the secrets master key and the customer's model key, and Railway's private network behind it.

| Issue | 1.0 | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| #708 WebSocket keeps streaming after a membership or key is revoked | **IN** (S) | Removing a departed teammate is the only offboarding control a small team has, so it has to work |
| #709 SSRF through company import preview | **IN** (S) | On a hosted box, a server-side fetch can reach cloud metadata and Railway-internal addresses. Separate Railway projects limit the blast radius; they don't close the hole |
| #710 Routine edits bypass the standing-instruction guard | **IN** (S) | It lets a prompt-injected agent rewrite future autonomous work. That is a persistence path on a box that runs untrusted content |
| #719 Members can set host-executed adapter command, env or cwd | **IN** (S) | The host is our container, holding the master key and the customer's model key. Hermes-only makes the fix simple: pin the command to the managed profile wrapper and refuse member-set command, env and cwd |
| #707 Restricted projects leak through activity, the WebSocket, export and routines | OUT | Restricted projects are an ACL *inside* one customer's team. For a 2 to 15 person team at 1.0, the guide says they are not a hard boundary. Revisit when a customer relies on them |
| #711 Member-visible metadata: private connections, secrets management, `tasks:assign_scope` | OUT | Connectors are out on hosted boxes, and secret management by members stays inside one customer. P2 |
| #712 Central visibility policy, WebSocket scoping, Postgres RLS | OUT | This is the roadmap for shared tenancy (#623 Phase 3), which 1.0 does not do |
| #720 Gmail read and draft routes use a colleague's private connection | OUT, with a condition | Hosted boxes configure no Gmail OAuth client, so the routes have no connection to misuse. It becomes IN the day connectors ship on hosted boxes |

## 7. What MK taught us that changes the product

1. **Stewards will not live on a board; they will live in their assistant.** Four of MK's open requests are ways to decide without opening the web UI: #540, #542, #544 and #546. So did the iMessage CEO agent (AGE-39) and the in-harness inbox (#662). → The assistant is the product surface, and the Teams epic is cut.
2. **The address was broken more often than the system.** `INVALID_ORIGIN` from a wrong public port cost a day (#483). Approval links used `127.0.0.1` (#539). `.local` names fail over a VPN (AGE-5, #547). The connect page's default address was reversed on field evidence (PR #653). → One public HTTPS name per box, and every link is built from `publicBaseUrl` (spec §5).
3. **"Configured" is not "applied".** Hermes agents ran with no mandate (AGE-8, #554). Directives were stored and never injected (AGE-2). The UI reported the wrong model (AGE-1). Agents were born with heartbeat off (v2026.902.0). → Every 1.0 surface reports what actually ran, and a hosted agent that cannot get its own Hermes profile fails instead of running on a shared one (R6).
4. **Metering was blind, and it cost a day of outage.** 467 of 467 runs on 3199 recorded 0 tokens, while one no-op heartbeat session burned about 10M input tokens a day and exhausted the Z.AI weekly quota on 2026-09-21 (observability plan §3). → With Hermes as the only runtime, OBS-1 and the 5M ceiling are launch-critical. We show tokens, never dollars.
5. **Install and upgrade are the product for a small team.** The first install took 90 to 120 founder minutes on site. The v2026.827.2 upgrade was rehearsed with the verdict NO-PROMOTION, and MK is still on its July commit (AGE-39, `doc/customers/MINIMUM-REPEATABLE-DEPLOYMENT.md` §5). → Managed boxes where we own the upgrade. On-prem becomes the self-hosted path.
6. **Questions that reach no one become silent decisions.** The Hermes clarify channel reached nobody, and a 120-second timeout let the agent decide by default (AGE-13, fixed fail-closed in #571). → Every question an agent asks surfaces in `list_pending_decisions`, and gated actions are enforced on the server, not trusted to the client (spec §7.2; Muse runs its tool calls as Python in a sandbox, #674).
7. **State that lies erodes trust faster than missing features.** An inbox that can never reach zero (#553). Agents created by agents that were "stewarded" with no steward (#550). A 403 reported as "this person does not exist" (#552). A PATCH that silently discarded `steward` (#551). → Pillar 2 gates are about truth, not new features.
8. **The product profile was a hidden fork.** MK ran `agentdash_mk`, while a company without an MK code gets `default`, where the workforce surfaces return 404 "with nothing explaining why" (`FirstRunStart.tsx:11`). → 1.0 settles it: hosted boxes are `default` only, and the MK stack stays on-prem.
